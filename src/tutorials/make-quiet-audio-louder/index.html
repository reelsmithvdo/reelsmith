How to Make Quiet Video Audio Louder (and Clearer)

November 8, 2026 · Tutorials · 3 min read

Boost low volume in a video, even out loud and quiet parts, remove background noise and separate the audio from the picture — free in your browser.

A great shot with sound you can barely hear is a common problem with phone and camera footage. Here is how to make audio louder in a video with Reelsmith, from the quickest fix to a polished, even result — free, in your browser.

Quick fix: raise the volume

  1. Click the clip on the timeline.
  2. In Effect Controls ▸ Audio, raise Volume. 100 % is the original level; you can go up to 300 %.
  3. Play it back and watch the meters on the right of the timeline. Keep the peaks below 0 dB — if the top of the meter turns red, the sound is close to distorting.

Prefer exact numbers? Select the clip and press G for Clip ▸ Audio Gain… to add or remove decibels. +6 dB is roughly twice as loud to the ear.

Better: make audio louder evenly with Normalize Loudness

Different clips are often recorded at different levels. Select them and choose Clip ▸ Normalize Loudness…, then pick a target:

  • −14 LUFS — YouTube, Spotify and most online video.
  • −16 LUFS — Apple Podcasts and Apple Music.
  • −23 LUFS — European broadcast (EBU R 128).
  • −24 LUFS — US broadcast.

Reelsmith measures each clip with the standard broadcast method and sets its Audio Gain, so every clip has the same perceived loudness and viewers don't have to keep reaching for the volume.

Boost audio without distortion: even out loud and quiet parts

If some words are loud and others quiet, drag Effects ▸ Audio Effects ▸ Compressor onto the clip. It turns down the loudest moments so you can raise the overall level without distortion. Use Make-up gain to bring it back up.

Tip: Order matters: compress first, then normalize. The compressor evens out the speech, and normalizing sets the final level.

Clean up the sound

Making audio louder also makes the noise louder, so clean it up. In Effects ▸ Audio Effects:

  • High-Pass removes rumble from traffic, wind and handling noise (80 Hz is a good start for voices).
  • DeHum removes the 50 or 60 Hz buzz from electrical equipment.
  • Noise Gate silences the hiss between words.
  • Parametric EQ — lift the highs a little for clearer speech, or cut the lows to reduce boominess.

Work on the audio separately

Video and audio from the same file are linked, so they move together. To edit the sound on its own — for example to boost it, move it, or delete it — select the clip and press ⌘L (Ctrl+L on Windows), or right-click ▸ Unlink. You don't need to unlink just to change the volume, though: selecting the clip is enough.

Tip: Recording a narration? Click the mic button on an audio track to record a voice-over straight into the timeline.

Music under speech

Put background music on its own track, select it and apply Effects ▸ Smart Audio ▸ Auto Duck Music Under Voice. The music dips by 14 dB automatically whenever someone speaks, with smooth fades you can adjust.

Make audio louder on a phone

The same tools work in the mobile editor. Tap the clip, then open the Edit sheet at the bottom of the screen and raise Volume. In addition, use headphones while checking: phone speakers hide low-frequency rumble and make quiet speech sound louder than it is.

A quick recipe for clear, loud speech

  1. Unlink the audio if you want to work on it alone (⌘L).
  2. Add High-Pass at 80 Hz and, if you hear a buzz, DeHum.
  3. Add a Compressor.
  4. Run Normalize Loudness… at −14 LUFS.
  5. Duck the music with Auto Duck Music Under Voice.

Frequently asked questions

How do I increase the volume of a video beyond 100%?

Raise Volume up to 300 % in Effect Controls, or add decibels with Audio Gain… (G). Watch the meters so the peaks stay below 0 dB.

Why does my audio distort when I make it louder?

The loudest moments are hitting the limit. Add a Compressor first, then raise the level, or lower the gain until the meters stop turning red.

What loudness should I use for YouTube?

−14 LUFS. YouTube turns louder videos down to about this level anyway, so going louder only adds distortion.

Is my audio uploaded?

No. All processing happens in your browser on your own computer.
