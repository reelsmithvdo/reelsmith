How to Edit YouTube Shorts: A Free Step-by-Step Workflow

November 14, 2026 · Editing tips · 5 min read

Edit a YouTube Short from raw clips to upload in about fifteen minutes: template, tight cuts, hook title, captions, effects, clean sound and a 9:16 export — free, in your browser.

Want to edit YouTube Shorts that people actually watch to the end? This guide takes one Short from raw phone clips to a finished upload in about fifteen minutes, using Reelsmith — a free editor that runs in your browser, with no upload and no watermark. Every step uses a real button in the editor, so you can follow along.

The workflow works the same for Instagram Reels and TikTok, because all three use the same vertical 9:16 format.

Before you edit YouTube Shorts: plan in one sentence

Good Shorts are about one thing. Before you open the editor, finish this sentence: "By the end, the viewer will…" — know a trick, see a result, laugh at a reaction. Everything that doesn't serve that sentence gets cut. As a result, the edit becomes much faster, because you already know what to keep.

Step 1: Start from the Shorts template

  1. Open the editor. On the start screen, under Start from a template, click Shorts / Reels / TikTok.
  2. You get a 1080×1920 sequence with a hook title, a Progress Bar and a Follow Button already placed. V1 is left empty for your video.
  3. Click Import video in the guide (or drag files onto the window) and drag your clips onto V1.

The template keeps the vertical frame even if you drop in landscape footage. Prefer to start from scratch? How to make vertical videos explains the frame settings.

Step 2: Cut it tight

Shorts are judged in the first seconds, so the edit should never pause.

  • Press C for the razor tool and click at the start and end of every pause or stumble.
  • Press V, select the piece you don't want and press ⇧Delete (Ripple Delete) — the gap closes on its own.
  • Move your best moment to the very start. For example, show the finished result first and explain how afterwards.

Tip: Zoom the timeline with = and − to cut word by word. The audio waveform shows exactly where each pause is.

Step 3: Write the hook title

The template's title says WAIT FOR IT. Double-click it in the Program monitor and type your own hook — three to six words that promise the payoff, such as "I fixed it in 10 seconds". Then click Safe under the monitor: keep the text inside the inner box, because YouTube's buttons and caption cover the edges and the bottom of a Short.

Want a different look? Select the title and double-click a style in Effects ▸ Text Styles, or read how to make animated text and 3D titles.

Step 4: Add captions so it works without sound

Many people scroll with the sound off. Captions keep them watching, and they make your Short accessible.

  1. Open the Captions tab and click Auto-generate. Speech recognition runs on your own device, so your audio is never uploaded.
  2. Click any caption to fix names and brand words.
  3. The template already sets large captions with a dark box, placed above YouTube's buttons. Change them with Style… if you like.

More detail: how to add subtitles to a video automatically.

Step 5: Add energy with effects and sound

A Short should change something on screen every few seconds. You don't need much:

  • Zoom Punch (beat) from Effects ▸ Creative Effects adds a quick push-in on a beat — great on a punchline.
  • A Whip Pan or Zoom In transition between two clips hides a cut and keeps the pace up.
  • Effects ▸ Sound Effects has original sounds like Whoosh, Pop and Ding. Double-click one to add it at the playhead.
  • Landscape clip that leaves empty space? Drag Background Blur Fill onto it instead of cropping.

Step 6: Make the sound clean and even

Viewers forgive a shaky shot, but not bad audio. In addition, YouTube turns loud videos down, so there is no benefit in being louder than everyone else.

  • Select your clips and choose Clip ▸ Normalize Loudness… at −14 LUFS (YouTube, Spotify and most online video).
  • Put music on its own audio track, select it and apply Effects ▸ Smart Audio ▸ Auto Duck Music Under Voice — the music dips whenever you speak.

Quiet phone audio? See how to make audio louder and clearer.

Step 7: Export your YouTube Short

  1. Click Export and choose the YouTube Shorts / Reels / TikTok (9:16) preset — 1080×1920 H.264 MP4.
  2. If you burned captions in during step 4 they are included; you can also save an .srt file.
  3. Upload the MP4 from the YouTube app or YouTube Studio. YouTube lists its current length and format rules in Upload YouTube Shorts.

Tip: Export takes seconds on most computers because Reelsmith encodes on your own graphics hardware — nothing is uploaded until you upload it yourself.

A checklist to edit YouTube Shorts that keep viewers

  • The first second shows the result or asks a question.
  • No pauses left; the picture changes every few seconds.
  • It makes sense with the sound off.
  • Text and captions sit inside the safe zone.
  • Voice is clear and the music sits underneath it.
  • It ends cleanly — a loop back to the start often gets replays.

Frequently asked questions

Can I edit YouTube Shorts on my phone?

Yes. Reelsmith works in mobile browsers with touch gestures, so you can edit and export on the same phone you filmed with. Generating captions is slower on a phone, so start with the Tiny or Base caption model.

Do I need a paid YouTube Shorts editor?

No. Everything in this guide — the template, captions, effects, loudness and the 9:16 export — is free in Reelsmith, with no watermark and no account.

Can I reuse a horizontal YouTube video as a Short?

Yes. Cut the best 15–45 seconds, then either scale the clip up to fill the 9:16 frame or apply Background Blur Fill to keep the whole picture.

Is my video uploaded while I edit?

No. Editing, captions and export run on your device. Read why your videos never leave your computer.
