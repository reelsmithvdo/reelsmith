How to Turn a Long Video into Shorts (Free, in Your Browser)

November 15, 2026 · Editing tips · 5 min read

Turn one long video into several Shorts: mark the best moments, give each its own vertical sequence, fill the frame, add captions and export them all — free, with nothing uploaded.

One good long video can become five or ten Shorts. This guide shows how to turn a long video into Shorts — a podcast, a tutorial, a stream or a vlog — by marking the best moments, cutting each one into its own vertical sequence and exporting them in one sitting. It all happens in Reelsmith, a free editor in your browser, so even a two-hour recording is never uploaded anywhere.

Repurposing works because the hard part, recording something worth watching, is already done. In addition, Shorts bring new viewers to your long videos, so each clip should make people curious about the full episode.

What makes a good moment when you repurpose long videos

Look for parts that make sense on their own, without the rest of the video:

  • One clear tip or answer — "the one setting that fixed my audio".
  • A strong reaction — surprise, a laugh, a disagreement.
  • A before-and-after or a result you can show in the first second.
  • A bold statement that people want to comment on.

Aim for 15–45 seconds each. If a moment needs a long introduction, it is probably not a Short — and that is fine, because the goal when you turn a long video into Shorts is quality, not quantity.

Step 1: Import the long video and mark the best moments

  1. Open the editor, import your recording and drag it onto the timeline.
  2. Play through it. Whenever something good happens, press M to add a marker at the playhead.
  3. Double-click a marker in the ruler to name it — for example Best tip or Funny moment — and give it a colour.
  4. Press ⇧M to jump from one marker to the next when you come back to them.

Tip: Press L twice to play at double speed while you look for moments; K stops. Long recordings go much faster this way.

Step 2: Make a vertical sequence for each Short

  1. Choose Sequence ▸ New Sequence… and name it after the moment, for example Short 1 — Best tip.
  2. Open Sequence ▸ Sequence Settings…, pick Vertical 9:16 (1080×1920) and untick Match frame size to the first video clip. Otherwise the landscape footage would switch the sequence back to 16:9.
  3. Click Apply. The new sequence opens as a tab above the timeline, next to your full video.

Step 3: Bring the moment into the Short

  1. Double-click the long video in the Project panel to open it in the Source monitor.
  2. Find the moment, click inside the Source monitor and press I at the start and O at the end.
  3. Click the timeline of your Short sequence and press , (Insert). Only the marked part is added.

Each Short has its own tab, so you can switch between them while you work. More detail: the Source monitor and multiple sequences.

Step 4: Fill the vertical frame

A landscape clip sits in the middle of a 9:16 frame with empty space above and below. You have two good options:

  • Crop in: select the clip and tick Scale to fill frame in Effect Controls ▸ Motion, then drag the picture so the speaker's face is centred.
  • Keep everything: drag Background Blur Fill from Effects ▸ Creative Effects onto the clip. The whole picture stays visible over a blurred copy — ideal for screen recordings and wide shots with two people.

The blurred background tool and the vertical video guide show both in more detail.

Step 5: Tighten it and add a hook

  • Cut the warm-up. Start on the most interesting sentence, even if it is mid-thought.
  • Remove pauses with the razor tool (C) and Ripple Delete (⇧Delete).
  • Press T and type a short hook at the top, such as "Nobody tells you this". Click Safe under the monitor to keep it clear of YouTube's buttons.

Step 6: Add captions to every Short

Open the Captions tab and click Auto-generate with Entire sequence selected. Each Short gets its own captions, made on your device. Then click Style… for larger text with a box, and move them up with the margin so they sit above the app's controls. See how to add subtitles automatically.

Step 7: Export all your Shorts

  1. Click a Short's tab, then Export, and choose YouTube Shorts / Reels / TikTok (9:16).
  2. Name the file after the moment and click Export.
  3. Repeat for each tab. Exports run on your own computer, so each one usually takes seconds.
  4. Upload them from the YouTube app or YouTube Studio — YouTube lists its current rules in Upload YouTube Shorts.

Tip: Upload the Shorts over several days rather than all at once, and mention the full video in each one — for example "full episode on my channel".

A quicker way to turn a long video into Shorts (one clip)

Need just one Short? Skip the extra sequences: set In and Out points on the long video's timeline with I and O, and choose Range ▸ Sequence In/Out in the export settings. Pair it with the YouTube Shorts / Reels / TikTok (9:16) preset and Background Blur Fill for a fast result.

Frequently asked questions

How many Shorts can I make from one long video?

As many as it has self-contained moments. A one-hour podcast often gives five to ten good clips; a ten-minute tutorial perhaps two or three.

Can I turn a long video into Shorts without cropping out people?

Yes. Use Background Blur Fill, which keeps the whole frame visible, or place two clips above each other for a split screen.

Is there a length limit when I repurpose long videos?

No server limit, because the file opens from your own computer. Very long 4K recordings are smoother on a computer with plenty of memory; proxies help on slower machines.

Do the Shorts need their own captions?

Yes — generate them per Short. It is quick, and each clip then works with the sound off.
