Short videos live or die in the first few seconds. These seven habits make a noticeable difference, and each one takes less than a minute in Reelsmith.

## 1. Start with the most interesting moment
Don't warm up. Open on the result, the punchline or the question your video answers. Drag your best clip to the start of the timeline, or trim the slow opening with the razor tool (`C`).

## 2. Cut every pause
Silences that feel natural in conversation feel slow on screen. Play through, split at the start and end of each pause, and delete the gap. The waveform on audio clips shows you where the pauses are. See [[delete-gaps|Deleting clips and closing gaps]].

## 3. Add captions
Many people watch with the sound off. [Generate captions automatically](/tutorials/add-subtitles-to-video-automatically/), correct any wrong words, and choose **Burn into the video** when you export.

## 4. Put a headline on screen
A few bold words in the first second tell viewers why they should stay. Press `T`, type, and apply a style from **Effects ▸ Text Styles**. A **Words Pop** animation draws the eye without being distracting. More in [How to make animated text and 3D titles](/tutorials/animated-text-titles/).

## 5. Change the picture every few seconds
Cut to a different angle, zoom in on the action, or add a quick transition such as **Whip Pan** or **Zoom In**. Even on a single shot, slowly keyframing **Scale** from 100 % to 110 % adds energy. See [[effect-controls|Effect Controls and keyframes]].

## 6. Make the sound clean and consistent
Viewers forgive average video, but not bad audio. Use **Clip ▸ Normalize Loudness…** so every clip is equally loud, and **Effects ▸ Smart Audio ▸ Auto Duck Music Under Voice** so music never drowns out speech. Our [audio tutorial](/tutorials/make-quiet-audio-louder/) covers the rest.

## 7. Frame for vertical screens
Keep faces and text in the middle third of a 9:16 frame, where app buttons and captions won't cover them. Click **Safe** in the Program monitor to see the margins. The [vertical video tutorial](/tutorials/vertical-video-for-shorts-reels-tiktok/) shows how to reframe landscape footage.

Put these together and even a simple phone clip feels intentional. [Open the editor](/) and try them on your next video.
